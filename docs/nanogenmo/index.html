NaNoGenMo

rules of NaNoGenMo

The only rule is that you share at least one novel and also your source code at the end.

The source code does not have to be licensed in a particular way, so long as you share it. The code itself does not need to be on GitHub, either. We’re just using this repo as a place to organize the community. (Convenient because many programmers have GitHub accounts and the Issues section works like a forum with excellent syntax highlighting.)

The “novel” is defined however you want. It could be 50,000 repetitions of the word “meow”. It could literally grab a random novel from Project Gutenberg. It doesn’t matter, as long as it’s 50k+ words.

Please try to respect copyright. We’re not going to police it, as ultimately it’s on your head if you want to just copy/paste a Stephen King novel or whatever, but the most useful/interesting implementations are going to be ones that don’t engender lawsuits.

Computational Art Journals

=> SEEDS Magazine, issue 1, 2, 3, 4

a magazine on generative art and software.

=> The HTML Review

The HTML Review is an annual journal of literature made to exist on the web. It is edited by Shelby Wilson and Maxwell Neely-Cohen.

=> Taper

an online literary magazine for small computational pieces, published by Bad Quarto

=> ORAL.PUB

ORAL.pub publishes poetry/art made for and inseparable from the web. ORAL.pub sites take advantage of their medium; their modes of interaction are meaningful and defy our internet instincts. ORAL.pub sites are often translated or multilingual.

=> Random Walk

Each issue features new computational media, responding to the particular constraints of presenting work within a web browser.

Ten Questions Concerning Generative Computer Art

“The idea becomes a machine that makes the art.” — Sol LeWitt, 1967

by Jon McCormack, Oliver Bown, Alan Dorin, Jonathan McCabe, Gordon Monro and Mitchell Whitelaw0

In this paper we pose ten questions we consider the most important for understanding generative computer art. For each question, we briefly discuss the implications and suggest how it might form the basis for further discussion.
